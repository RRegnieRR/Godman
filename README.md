## Project structure

- `index.html` — modular development entry point.
- `styles/game.css` — interface styling.
- `src/game.js` — game world, controls, physics, terrain, and rendering.
- `src/config.js` — rendering and atmosphere quality settings.
- `src/runtime/adaptive-quality.js` — reversible frame-rate based resolution control.
- `src/runtime/running-gait.js` — smooth playback of the suit's baked athletic run.
- `src/data/athletic-run.js` — suit bone poses prepared from the athletic reference.
- `tools/bake-athletic-run.cjs` — offline retargeting, clearance, and contact preparation.
- `src/runtime/atmosphere.js` — close-range atmospheric haze and mountain depth.
- `src/runtime/planetary-sky.js` — daylight scattering, twilight, planet-specific haze, and curved cloud decks.
- `src/runtime/physical-lighting.js` — inverse-square stellar illumination, blackbody light color, and eye-adapted exposure.
- `src/runtime/deep-sky.js` — procedural volumetric nebulae, stellar clusters, and navigable 3D galaxies with atmospheric extinction.
- `src/data/nasa-exoplanets.js` — bundled NASA Exoplanet Archive snapshot for additional real planetary systems.
- `assets/earth/` — Earth textures, elevation data, and mountain heightmaps.
- `assets/character/` — the rigged, textured astronaut GLB used by the player plus its attribution notice.
- `vendor/` — pinned Three.js r128 runtime, GLTF loader, and its MIT license.
- `solar-jumper.html` — preserved single-file snapshot from before modularization.

The preserved snapshot is intentionally not used by `index.html`, so future work can change the modular game without altering that copy.

Terrain distance can be changed in Settings. The default 11 × 11 streamed area can be reduced to 7 × 7 or expanded to 15 × 15; outer chunks use progressively lighter geometry while nearby terrain keeps full detail.

Earth terrain rings are committed atomically: the current complete ring stays visible while replacement chunks and LODs stream in, then the renderer swaps only after the replacement is complete. Chunk borders include small geometry skirts, the cutaway follows Earth rotation every frame, and streaming uses the post-physics player position to avoid airborne and landing gaps.

Target locks include predictive arrival assistance. The controller uses closing speed and remaining distance to begin braking before a high-speed intercept, align lateral motion, and settle onto planets at walking-speed touchdown. Locked stars use the same guidance but stop at a safe observation radius instead of treating the photosphere as land.

The orbital laser uses a terrain-independent thermal damage path. Its beam and rotating impact plasma respond immediately, heat decays after releasing the trigger, and sustained contact vaporizes the planet without rebuilding Earth mountains or producing fallback-blue landing chunks.

The modular build includes a cinematic adaptive quality tier, animated stellar surfaces, wavelength-inspired atmospheric scattering, eclipse-aware illumination, shoreline-faded reflective water, speed-responsive camera optics, and higher-resolution celestial meshes. Intergalactic travel uses capped, distance-aware galaxy populations plus a single-batch, camera-centred cosmic backdrop, keeping the universe visually continuous without a reachable boundary or multi-million-point frame spike. The playable character now uses a genuinely rigged and textured humanoid spacesuit with a 94-bone skeleton, anatomically separate five-finger hands, and embedded `idle`, `moon_walk`, `floating`, and `wave` animation clips. The imported idle take provides breathing and finger detail, while a shared position-driven controller owns the four limb chains. Walking and running use separate contact timing, stride lengths limited by actual leg reach, arm counter-swing, and a running flight phase. Ground movement remains capped at 1.8 m/s walking and 4.4 m/s running. Charged jumps retain the original camera-directed planetary launch range so a full jump can leave Earth. Charge and touchdown lower the body while inverse kinematics maintains foot contact; takeoff extends the legs and swings the arms up. Atmospheric falling, relaxed zero-g coasting, swimming, powered flight, turning, and braking have distinct blended targets. Knees and elbows use explicit bend planes, hands follow their forearms, and solved joint changes have a bounded angular rate. Whole-body attitude follows thrust at a bounded turn rate rather than obtaining every direction through shoulder/hip overextension. Coasting in space does not automatically self-right the body. Repulsors are attached at the palms and soles, with forearm roll and limited wrist adjustment to orient the palms; the soft tapering exhaust follows each actual emitter normal, responds to throttle, and uses one shared illumination light. These are fictional Iron Man-inspired propulsion visuals, not a physically exact human flight simulation. The material is rendered locally with the orange pressure-suit palette and dark visor from the project's visual direction, while the lightweight procedural astronaut remains only as an offline fallback. Its bundled catalogue now contains 26 NASA-derived stellar systems and 115 archived exoplanets, in addition to the hand-built Solar System and nearby destinations. The newest system parameters were refreshed from the [NASA Exoplanet Archive PSCompPars TAP service](https://exoplanetarchive.ipac.caltech.edu/docs/TAP/usingTAP.html) on 2026-09-01 so the game remains fully usable offline.

The deep sky includes ray-marched emission, planetary, and supernova-remnant nebulae plus procedural open and globular star clusters. The Solar System now begins 26,670 light-years from Sagittarius A* inside a 104,000-light-year Milky Way. Its 12-million-sample deterministic catalogue uses a rotated five-kiloparsec box/peanut bar, unequal logarithmic Scutum–Centaurus, Perseus, Sagittarius–Carina, and Norma–Outer arms, a finite Local Arm spur, exponential thin and thick disks, an oblate stellar halo, and flared and warped outskirts. Rendering uses 1.35 million resident galactic samples inside the disk and up to 2.6 million for the exterior silhouette. The former observer-wrapped Milky Way sky overlay has been removed completely; the physical galaxy and a camera-centred 900,000-star field now supply the entire sky without a reachable wall, lattice, band, seam, fade transition, or empty rectangular sectors.

Deep-sky colour follows approximate visible black-body stellar classes: numerous but faint red/orange M and K stars, warm G stars, blue-white F/A stars, and rare luminous blue stars. The Galactic bulge is warmer and older, spiral arms retain younger blue populations, and dust reddening plus H-alpha-like star-forming regions add localized red structure. Galaxy brightness is compensated across streamed detail levels and alpha-composited so loading more stellar samples adds detail without washing every dense region to white.

There are 100 navigable 3D galaxies in total: the Milky Way, six named nearby galaxies, and 93 deterministic deep-field systems arranged in filament-like distance groups rather than on a boundary sphere. Their combined visual catalogues contain tens of millions of stellar samples and metadata estimates for the much larger physical populations. Real catalog stars retain their known names; each Milky Way GPU sample also has a stable `MW-########` designation that can be entered in the destination finder. Reaching 10 AU from a numbered star deterministically creates 3–8 varied planets and their orbit guides; passing 20 AU removes their meshes, labels, orbits, and physics bodies. Beyond the catalogue lies progressively emptier space rather than a physical universe edge. These objects do not use photographs, canvas images, or flat billboards.

Sagittarius A* uses a live Kerr-aware null-geodesic lens tuned to the `a/M = 0.6` presentation used for Gargantua in *Interstellar*. A rendered copy of the real scene is reprojected through observer-distance-aware curved ray paths, so stars, nebulae, galaxies, and nearby objects are genuinely lensed instead of replaced with a decorative texture. The projected spin shifts and compresses the capture shadow while a bounded gravitomagnetic term drags near-hole rays in the prograde direction. Escaping rays integrate through a geometrically thin disk beginning near the prograde ISCO; Doppler beaming, gravitational frequency shift, multiple disk crossings, and near-critical paths produce the asymmetric direct and secondary images. The integrator uses adaptive midpoint steps, a 1280-pixel capture ceiling, and adaptive trace counts to contain the GPU cost. Horizon crossing is one-way and no longer teleports the player into a fictional mirrored universe.

## Planetary skies

The game now renders a directional sky behind the world instead of trying to change the renderer clear colour underneath a fixed scene background. Local solar elevation, eclipses, observer altitude, and planetary rotation drive daylight and night. Earth uses Rayleigh/Mie single scattering with a stratospheric ozone absorption band, a brighter horizon, warm low-Sun light, and dark nights. Distant stars fade in daylight and reappear continuously toward night and space. Surface haze fades out well below orbit so it does not wash the globe into a flat colour.

Clouds are two procedural curved decks on Earth, with density-based lighting, wind, co-rotation, and depth occlusion against terrain. The same decks remain visible when viewed from above. Mars uses dusty scattering and a blue low-Sun aureole with sparse high ice clouds; Venus and Titan use dense coloured haze and cloud cover; the gas/ice giants have their own cloud-top profiles. Airless worlds retain black skies. Unknown atmospheres use the world's declared colour as a visual fallback. These are physically motivated real-time approximations, not measured weather or a full multiple-scattering climate simulation.

Open [the local sky studio](http://127.0.0.1:4173/sky-lab.html) to inspect Earth, Mars, Venus, Titan, or airless worlds at day, sunset, night, and different altitudes. It uses the same sky renderer as the game and needs no pointer lock. Run `node tests/planetary-sky.test.js` for the state, cloud-depth/curvature, eclipse, altitude, haze, and lighting checks. Camera rays are reconstructed away from the far clipping plane so they remain finite with the game's astronomical view range.

Physical visual references: [NASA: why Earth's sky is blue](https://spaceplace.nasa.gov/blue-sky/en/) and [NASA: Martian sunrises and sunsets](https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/planets/mars/what-does-a-sunrise-sunset-look-like-on-mars/).

## Continuous terrain boundaries

The walking terrain uses a common physical height-field normal sampler across all detail levels. Fine edges interpolate onto their coarse neighbours, with a short smooth transition band and matching colour/lighting. Crack covers follow the final surface. Surface colour is bilinearly sampled; fine ground tone is evaluated per pixel in shared planet coordinates, with distance filtering instead of vertex-density-dependent speckles. The global globe and mountain overlays use the same world-space cutaway as the walking terrain, and polar cells render one LOD each. Deformation reapplies the shared normals and edge transitions using double-precision source radii.

Run `node tests/terrain-surface.test.js` and `node tests/terrain-streaming.test.js` to verify shared edges/normals, LOD changes and corners, crater deformation, rotating cutaways, cached streaming and polar selection.
